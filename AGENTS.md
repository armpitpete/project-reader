# Project Reader Codex Rules

The Project Reader exists to help ordinary non-technical readers understand: what a project is, what is complete, what remains, how complete the defined work is, how likely the current milestone is to finish, why the assessment should be trusted, how the project was made, and how the owner can be contacted.

Keep the first screen calm and simple. Put evidence, technology detail, and scoring explanations in optional deeper layers.

Every public factual claim, status, score, recommendation, technology claim, and link must trace to collected evidence. Missing, stale, partial, conflicting, or unsupported authority must produce an honest unknown state, not a numeric completion or likelihood result.

Do not treat absence of a detected problem as positive evidence. Completed work is not automatically recent progress, delivery history, repository health, or blocker manageability. Open issues, pull requests, commits, and activity do not automatically define completion.

Preserve partial stage counts mathematically. Do not collapse partially completed work into a simple done/not-done item.

Technology manifests may prove that a technology exists. They do not automatically prove why it was chosen, what it does in this project, or where it is used. Keep generic explanations separate from project-specific evidence.

Links supplied through evidence or command-line options must use an explicitly permitted safe scheme and destination form.

Before returning a candidate, inspect the governing contracts and tests, implement the bounded outcome, run positive, negative, and adversarial tests, inspect the full diff as a hostile reviewer, correct unsupported claims and unsafe inputs, rerun tests and proof generation, check for unexplained generated-file drift, inspect CI, and continue through safe corrections.
