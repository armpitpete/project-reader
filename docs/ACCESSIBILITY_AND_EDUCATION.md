# Accessibility and Education v0.1

## Reading design

- Use ordinary words and concrete examples.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use a predictable order on every project.
- Keep important information visible without opening menus.
- Explain abbreviations at first use.
- Let readers increase text size and spacing.
- Do not rely on colour alone.
- Do not require timed interaction.
- Respect reduced-motion settings.
- Support keyboard and screen-reader navigation.

## Educational technology cards

Each card has five fixed prompts:

- **What is it?**
- **What does it do here?**
- **Why was it used?**
- **Where can I see it?**
- **Why does it matter?**

Reasons must be labelled as one of:

- documented by the owner;
- strongly supported by repository evidence;
- likely inference;
- unknown.

## Fun without overload

Allowed:

- a small project character or symbol;
- satisfying completion movement;
- technology connections revealed one at a time;
- tiny definitions on tap;
- simple “follow the journey” diagrams;
- optional discovery prompts.

Avoid:

- confetti on every action;
- flashing states;
- noisy badges;
- streak pressure;
- competitive scores;
- forced quizzes;
- hidden navigation.
