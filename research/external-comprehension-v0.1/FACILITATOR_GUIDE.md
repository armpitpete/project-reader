# Facilitator Guide

Purpose: collect directional evidence about whether real people understand and trust the deployed Project Reader surface. This is not a teaching session, a medical assessment, or a scientific study.

Session length: approximately 10-15 minutes.

Use one anonymous participant ID: `R01`, `R02`, `R03`, `R04`, or `R05`.

## Ground Rules

- Project Reader is being tested, not the participant.
- The participant may stop at any time without giving a reason.
- Record only broad self-described context: non-technical, young person, neurodivergent, or overlapping categories.
- Do not record names, email addresses, phone numbers, medical diagnoses, school or workplace names, or other unnecessary identifying details.
- Keep participant statements separate from facilitator interpretation.
- Do not explain the answers before the participant has tried the site.
- If help is requested, record the exact help requested and the help actually given.

## Setup

1. Open https://armpitpete.github.io/project-reader/.
2. Use a normal browser session. Do not sign in to GitHub.
3. Use only public repositories.
4. Have a blank record ready outside Git if you will write real session details.
5. Keep this guide visible only to the facilitator.

## Consent And Anonymity Wording

Read this before starting:

> I am testing Project Reader, not testing you. I want to see what makes sense, what is confusing, and whether the page gives enough trust evidence. I will use an anonymous ID such as R01, not your name. Please do not give me personal, medical, school, workplace or contact details. You can stop at any time, skip any question, or ask me to delete a note before we finish.

Ask:

1. Is it okay to take anonymous notes about what you say and do during this short session?
2. Do you understand that you can stop at any time?

If either answer is no, stop the session and record only that the session did not proceed.

## Before-Use Questions

Ask these before the participant uses the site:

1. When you see the name "Project Reader", what do you expect it might do?
2. How comfortable do you usually feel reading a GitHub project page?
3. If a tool explained a project to you, what would make you trust or distrust it?

Record verbatim answers or close transcription. Do not correct, teach or add context.

## Observation Prompts During Use

Give the participant the public URL and say:

> Please use this page as naturally as you can. Say what you are looking at and what you think it means. I may ask what you are trying to find, but I will not explain the page unless you ask for help.

Use only neutral prompts:

- What are you looking for now?
- What do you think this part means?
- What would you do next?
- Is there anything you want to open or ignore?
- What, if anything, feels unclear?

Do not ask leading prompts such as "Can you see the evidence section?" or "Do you understand that this means it is complete?"

## What To Observe

Record:

- whether the participant understands the first screen without opening details;
- whether they use optional disclosure controls;
- any hesitation, rereading, backtracking or visible confusion;
- any mistaken interpretation of completion, likelihood, evidence, technology, contact or limitations;
- any request for help;
- time to first useful answer about what the project is or what state it is in;
- whether the participant can identify at least one reason to trust or not trust the result.

Hesitation means a pause, repeated rereading, uncertainty, backtracking, or asking for confirmation. Do not treat quiet reading as hesitation unless the participant shows uncertainty or delay that affects the task.

Help requested means the participant asks for explanation, navigation help, technical help, or confirmation. Record the request before answering.

## After-Use Questions

Ask after the participant has used the page:

1. In your own words, what is the project?
2. What, if anything, seems already finished?
3. What, if anything, seems still unfinished or uncertain?
4. What evidence did the page give you for that answer?
5. What would you change to make the page easier to understand?
6. Would you trust this enough to use it for another public GitHub project? Why or why not?
7. Was there any point where you felt stuck, rushed, or unsure what to do?

Record the answers as participant statements. Add facilitator interpretation only in the separate interpretation fields after the session.

## Paid-Outcome Prompt Boundary

Use `PAID_OUTCOME_TEST.md` only after the participant has seen the public example and the exact deliverable description. Do not pressure, negotiate, create payment links, or reinterpret polite interest as commitment.
